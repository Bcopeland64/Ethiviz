# 2. Project Pain Curves Comparison Matrix

## Purpose

This matrix compares the current project pain profile with the target state after Ethiviz_V6 production readiness is established. It provides a practical view of where the organization experiences friction today and how the program will reduce that friction over time.

## Pain Curves Comparison Matrix

| Pain Area | Current State | Pain Level | Target State | Improvement Outcome |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| Product reliability | Platform behavior varies across environments and use cases | 9/10 | Stable, tested production environment | Reduced downtime and higher user confidence |
| Governance clarity | Decision rights and accountability may be diffuse | 8/10 | Formal RACI, steering committee, clear escalation paths | Faster decisions and fewer delays |
| Quality assurance | Validation may be incomplete or inconsistent | 8/10 | Regression and acceptance testing with sign-off gates | Higher output trust and fewer rework cycles |
| Security posture | Controls may not be fully defined or enforced | 9/10 | Hardened security, authentication, access, and secure deployment | Reduced breach exposure and compliance risk |
| Deployment confidence | Release processes may be ad hoc | 7/10 | Repeatable deployment and rollback procedures | Better release stability |
| Data handling and privacy | Risk around sensitive data and model outputs is unclear | 8/10 | Data classification, retention, and handling controls | Stronger operational compliance |
| User adoption | Stakeholders may not trust output consistency | 7/10 | Explainability, documentation, and support model | Improved acceptance and sustained adoption |
| Scope control | Features may expand without change control | 6/10 | Formal scope baseline and change approval structure | Reduced cost and schedule drift |
| Stakeholder visibility | Progress reporting may be uneven | 6/10 | Monthly steering reviews and operational dashboards | Better alignment and fewer surprises |
| Supportability | Post-launch support may be undefined | 7/10 | Runbooks, support model, monitoring, and ownership | Faster incident response |

## Interpretation

The pain curves show the greatest challenge is not technology alone; it is the lack of disciplined operational controls around reliability, governance, quality, and security. The highest-severity pain points are reliability, governance, and security readiness. These are the areas most likely to influence successful production launch and user trust.

## Target Pain Curve Profile

After production readiness controls are implemented, the program should reduce the biggest pain points to the following approximate level:

- Reliability: 2-3/10
- Governance: 2/10
- Security posture: 2-3/10
- Data handling: 3/10
- User trust: 3/10
- Supportability: 3/10

This reduction will materially improve confidence for launch, adoption, and sustained operations.

## Key Management Action

The project management team must prioritize early stabilization of the highest-pain areas before feature expansion. The organization should resist adding non-critical scope until the production baseline has proven reliability and governance maturity.
